P0014	STYLE=Heading 1	Abstract
P0015	STYLE=normal	In recent years, soybean seed decay has led to substantial yield and quality losses in tropical Brazil, yet the timing of weather conditions associated with damaged grains (DG) remains poorly understood. We analyzed DG percentages from 72 field trials, conducted during four growing seasons (2022/2023 to 2025/2026) in the states of Mato Grosso and Rondônia, using 80-day weather trajectories preceding the harvest date. Daily maximum and minimum temperature, relative humidity, and rainfall were obtained from ERA5 reanalysis data. Each weather variable was analyzed separately using season-adjusted linear regression across seven prespecified 10-day intervals and scalar-on-function regression. Log-transformed rainfall showed the strongest cross-method agreement. In the interval-based regression analysis, the largest positive estimate occurred during −50 to −41 days, whereas the scalar-on-function analysis identified a broader positive association extending from −60 to −40 days. Minimum temperature showed a more complex pattern, with positive associations earlier and negative associations later in the exposure domain, whereas relative humidity and maximum temperature were not reproduced as coherent functional patterns. The rainfall signal remained evident under several sensitivity analyses, although it weakened after exclusion of the highest-DG trials and varied in strength across leave-one-season-out analyses. Overall, the most consistent finding was a broad rainfall-associated period approximately 60 to 40 days before harvest, likely overlapping much of pod and seed development. These temporal signatures provide a biologically grounded framework for microclimate studies, validation in larger independent datasets and future development of prediction models.

P0018	STYLE=Heading 1	Introduction
P0019	STYLE=normal	Soybean (Glycine max (L.) Merr.) production in tropical regions of Brazil has recently faced increasing concern over seed decay symptoms, particularly in Mid-Northern Mato Grosso and Rondônia. This concern has emerged in the context of changes in soybean production systems, including earlier sowing dates to allow sufficient time for the establishment of a second crop of maize or cotton within the same agricultural season, as well as the adoption of new soybean cultivars with different agronomic and genetic backgrounds. 
P0020	STYLE=normal	Symptoms begin on pods at R5 and include water-soaked areas or dark green discoloration, with no apparent necrosis or pod opening. When opened, the pods contain seeds showing partial or complete decay, irregularly distributed among pods and seeds, which compromises grain quality (Kudlawiec et al. 2023). The symptoms resemble Diaporthe seed decay caused by Diaporthe species, although pod symptoms have been observed earlier than previously described in the literature (Petrović et al. 2021). In addition to Diaporthe, several other fungi have been isolated from symptomatic seeds and pods; species from the genera Colletotrichum, Fusarium, and Moniliophthora are frequently recovered from decayed seeds with or without symptoms; however, the relative contribution of these taxa to disease onset, progression, and final grain deterioration remains uncertain (Dos Santos et al. 2024; Nascimento et al. 2025; Ramos et al. 2026). 
P0021	STYLE=normal	Seed decay is an important constraint to soybean seed quality and production, as fungal infection and deterioration can reduce germination and seed vigor, impair seedling establishment, and compromise the physiological quality of stored seed (Brown et al. 1987; Gillen et al. 2012). In Brazil, soybean grain quality is regulated according to the occurrence and severity of defects such as moldy, fermented, germinated, and damaged grains. Soybean grain is commercially classified according to official quality standards, with a maximum tolerance of 8% for total damaged grains. Grain lots exceeding this threshold may be subject to commercial discounts, depending on the buyer and applicable contract (Brasil, 2007).
P0022	STYLE=normal	Weather may influence seed decay through multiple biological pathways. Atmospheric moisture and rainfall can affect fungal sporulation, dispersal of inoculum, infection, and subsequent seed colonization, whereas temperature and plant water status can influence host physiology, reproductive development, and the susceptibility of developing pods and seeds to deterioration (Shortt et al. 1981; Tekrony et al. 1983; Rupe and Ferriss 1986; Balducchi and McGee 1987; Westgate et al. 1989). These effects may also vary across crop development. Studies of the Diaporthe complex showed that infected soybean residue can serve as an important inoculum source and that rainfall promotes short-distance dispersal of conidia, while latent infections may become established in immature soybean tissues, including pods (Kmetz et al., 1979). At broader scales, rainfall during pod filling and crop maturation was strongly associated with subsequent seed decay (Shortt et al., 1981), while moisture-related conditions, particularly minimum relative humidity during seed development and maturation, were more consistently associated with seed infection than precipitation totals alone (TeKrony et al., 1983). Experimental and observational studies further indicate that the effect of moisture depends on developmental timing: wet periods beginning around flowering and extending into pod development have been associated with greater pod infection, whereas high pod water content, relative humidity, and favorable temperature near physiological maturity can promote seed infection and subsequent fungal growth (Lamka and McGee, 1986; Rupe and Ferriss, 1986; Balducchi and McGee, 1987). 
P0023	STYLE=normal	Weather conditions favoring infection during pod formation and grain filling may therefore differ from those that promote later colonization, symptom expression, or amplification of damage in previously infected or predisposed tissues. This distinction is particularly important in field datasets where the response is the final percentage of damaged grains rather than pathogen-specific incidence. The observed phenotype may integrate multiple processes occurring at different times, including infection, latent colonization, physiological stress, seed development, and late-season tissue deterioration. Latent Diaporthe infections may remain asymptomatic in immature tissues and become more evident as pods and seeds approach senescence and maturity, further separating the timing of infection from the timing of visible damage (Kmetz et al., 1979; Rupe and Ferriss, 1987). Consequently, weather associations detected retrospectively may reflect different stages of disease development rather than a single infection period.
P0024	STYLE=normal	Despite the economic significance of soybean grain deterioration, the relationship between weather dynamics during reproductive crop development and final grain damage remains poorly characterized in the tropics of Brazil. Lehner et al. (2025) reported approximately 1.7-fold greater cumulative precipitation during the reproductive stage in a late-planted trial that developed substantially more damaged grains. Their analysis, however, besides being based on a limited number of experiments, summarized rainfall across the entire reproductive period, providing limited information on when weather conditions may be most strongly associated with subsequent grain damage.
P0025	STYLE=normal	In this study, we examined weather conditions associated with soybean grain damage evaluated at harvest across 72 field experiments conducted in Mato Grosso and Rondônia, Brazil. We hypothesized that grain damage reflects temporally distinct environmental processes operating during reproductive development. Specifically, we expected an earlier period associated with infection establishment, followed by a subsequent period in which atmospheric moisture and thermal conditions may influence pathogen colonization and the expression of damage in previously infected or predisposed pods and seeds.
P0026	STYLE=normal	To test this hypothesis, we evaluated 80-day meteorological trajectories preceding grain assessment after harvest using complementary prespecified 10-day interval and scalar-on-function regression analyses, with the aim of identifying broad temporal patterns associated with final damaged-grain percentage. Particular attention was given to moisture-related variables (rainfall and relative humidity) and temperature because these variables may capture complementary components of atmospheric moisture and thermal conditions during crop reproductive development. Our primary objective was to determine which meteorological conditions, and at what times during reproductive development, were associated with final soybean grain damage. Given the relatively small number of experiments, analyses were deliberately parsimonious, with restricted model complexity, conservative smoothing, and explicit estimation of uncertainty.

P0028	STYLE=Heading 1	Materials and Methods
P0030	STYLE=Heading 2	Field trials dataset and response variable
P0031	STYLE=normal	The dataset comprised 72 field trials conducted across four growing seasons (2022/2023 to 2025/2026) in commercial soybean-producing municipalities in Mato Grosso (MT) and Rondônia (RO), Brazil (Fig. 1). These trials were conducted to evaluate the effect of fungicides or planting dates (Kudlawiec et al. 2023; Belufi et al. 2024; Belufi et al. 2025; Belufi et al. 2026; Farias-Neto et al. 2023; Farias-neto et al. 2024). We used data from two major commercial cultivars, considered equally susceptible to seed decay: Brasmax Desafio RR (8473RSF) (n = 52) and Brasmax Olimpo IPRO (80I82RSF IPRO) (n = 20). The interval from sowing to harvest ranged from 93 to 137 days, with a median of 118 days. The primary response variable was the mean percentage of damaged grains (DG) from the non fungicide-treated plots.  Samples of 25–125 g of grain per plot were collected after harvest and reduced using a sample divider or homogenizer. DGs were determined according to the Brazilian official grain classification standards, including burned, moldy, fermented, damaged, immature, shriveled, sprouted, and heat-damaged grains.

P0033	STYLE=Heading 2	Weather temporal series
P0034	STYLE=normal	Daily meteorological time series corresponding to the coordinates of each trial site were retrieved from the ERA5 reanalysis using the get_era5() function from the {r4pde} R package (Del Ponte 2026). Weather trajectories were aligned to a standardized relative time axis anchored to the recorded field evaluation date (t = 0). The primary analytical window comprised the 80 calendar days preceding harvest (t = −80 to −1), broadly spanning reproductive development from approximately pod/seed development (R3–R4) through harvest.

P0068	STYLE=Heading 2	Stability analyses
P0069	STYLE=normal	Stability analyses were designed to assess whether the estimated weather associations depended materially on specific analytical choices or characteristics of the dataset. All sensitivity analyses were specified before their corresponding results were examined and were used to stress-test the primary findings rather than as alternative analyses. The broad-interval and scalar-on-function models were refitted under each sensitivity scenario. Stability was evaluated based on process-level evidence, interval-specific effect magnitude and uncertainty, agreement between broad and functional analyses, preservation of effect direction and approximate timing, and sensitivity to omission of individual growing seasons.

P0082	STYLE=Heading 3	Leave-one-season-out analysis
P0083	STYLE=normal	Internal season stability was evaluated by fitting four additional models, each omitting one season. For each omission, both the broad-interval and scalar-on-function models were refitted using the remaining three seasons. The analysis examined whether the process-level association remained apparent, the direction of the principal interval coefficient was preserved, the functional curve retained a similar broad temporal location, and the omission of one season caused a substantial change or sign reversal. With only four seasons, each omission removed a substantial and potentially distinctive portion of the dataset. Consequently, changes in precision were expected. Greater concern was placed on major reversals in direction, disappearance of the temporal pattern, or movement of the association to a biologically different portion of the exposure domain.
P0084	STYLE=Heading 2	Residual diagnostics 
P0085	STYLE=normal	Model adequacy was evaluated separately for every season-adjusted broad-interval and scalar-on-function model using residual-versus-fitted and normal Q-Q plots. Residual-versus-fitted plots were examined for systematic curvature and changes in residual dispersion across fitted DG values. Normal Q-Q plots compared standardized residuals with theoretical normal quantiles to identify departures from the Gaussian residual assumption. These diagnostics were interpreted graphically and were not used to select meteorological processes or alter the temporal specification.
P0086	STYLE=Heading 2	Data analysis, availability and reproducibility
P0087	STYLE=normal	All analyses were conducted in R version 4.5.0 (R Core Team 2025). Broad-interval associations were estimated using Gaussian linear models, with overall contributions of the meteorological terms evaluated by partial F-tests using functions from the base R stats package. Scalar-on-function regression was implemented using methods available in the refund package, following the functional regression framework described by Goldsmith et al. (2011). Cubic B-spline basis functions for the temporal coefficient functions were constructed using the splines package, which is distributed with R. Uncertainty in the functional coefficient functions and integrated interval contrasts was quantified using the cluster-bootstrap procedure described above. All data processing, model fitting, sensitivity analyses, leave-one-season-out analyses, figure generation, and result verification were implemented in reproducible R scripts. The complete data and code required to reproduce the analyses are publicly available at the project repository: https://github.com/IgorErhardt/SeedDecay_TemporalWeatherSignal.

P0094	STYLE=Heading 3	\n10 day-interval regression analysis
P0095	STYLE=normal	Log-transformed rainfall showed the clearest and most consistent relationship with damaged grains (DG) in the 10-day interval analysis (Table 1). Positive associations were concentrated in the early-to-middle portion of the exposure window, particularly from −60 to −41 days, with the largest estimate during −50 to −41 days (Fig. 3A). The overall rainfall pattern was largely maintained after outcome transformation and aggregation by meteorological unit, but was attenuated after exclusion of the three observations with the highest DG values (Fig. 3A). Leave-one-season-out analyses also retained the general positive pattern in this portion of the exposure window, although the strength of the overall evidence varied among season omissions (Fig. 5A).
P0096	STYLE=normal	Relative humidity also showed an overall relationship with DG, but with contrasting effects across the exposure window (Table 1). Higher relative humidity was associated with lower DG during −40 to −31 days and higher DG during −20 to −11 days (Fig. 3B). The overall contribution of relative humidity was comparatively stable across sensitivity and leave-one-season-out analyses (Figs. 4B and 5B). However, the opposing interval-specific estimates indicate temporal heterogeneity and should be interpreted cautiously, particularly because a corresponding smooth temporal pattern was not supported by the scalar-on-function analysis.
P0097	STYLE=normal	Maximum temperature showed a similarly heterogeneous temporal pattern. Higher maximum temperature was associated with increased DG during −40 to −31 days but decreased DG during −20 to −11 days (Fig. 3C; Table 1). The overall association remained evident under the main sensitivity analyses, although its strength varied when individual seasons were omitted (Figs. 4C and 5C). The reversal in effect direction across intervals and sensitivity to season therefore limit a straightforward biological interpretation.
P0098	STYLE=normal	Evidence for minimum temperature was weaker and less stable. Positive and negative associations occurred at −70 to −61 and −20 to −11 days, respectively (Fig. 3D; Table 1), while estimates for the remaining intervals were imprecise. The overall relationship weakened after exclusion of the highest-DG observations and was not consistently retained in leave-one-season-out analyses (Figs. 4D and 5D), providing limited support for a reproducible minimum-temperature pattern.

P0110	STYLE=Heading 3	Scalar-on-function analysis
P0111	STYLE=normal	The scalar-on-function analysis identified a broad positive association between log-transformed rainfall and DG across the early-to-middle portion of the temporal domain (Fig. 6A; Table 2). The coefficient function was predominantly positive from approximately −60 to −40 days, with positive integrated contrasts across three adjacent 10-day intervals from −60 to −31 days. This pattern was broadly concordant with the prespecified interval analysis, supporting a relatively extended rainfall-associated period rather than a sharply defined daily window. The functional rainfall pattern was retained after outcome transformation and meteorological-unit aggregation, but weakened after exclusion of the three observations with the highest DG values (Fig. 7A). Leave-one-season-out analyses retained a recognizable coefficient shape, although the strength of the process-level evidence varied among seasons (Fig. 8A).
P0112	STYLE=normal	Relative humidity showed weaker evidence of a smooth temporal association with DG (Fig. 6B; Table 2). Although the coefficient function tended to be positive across the middle portion of the domain, none of the integrated 10-day contrasts had cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals excluding zero. The overall pattern was also less stable across sensitivity and leave-one-season-out analyses (Figs. 7B and 8B). Thus, the interval-specific relative-humidity associations identified in the prespecified analysis were not reproduced as a clearly supported smooth temporal pattern.
P0113	STYLE=normal	Maximum temperature showed little evidence of a coherent functional association with DG (Fig. 6C; Table 2). The coefficient function remained close to zero across most of the temporal domain, and none of the integrated contrasts were clearly supported. This absence of a functional signal persisted across the sensitivity and leave-one-season-out analyses (Figs. 7C and 8C), indicating that the opposing interval-specific maximum-temperature estimates from the prespecified analysis were not reproduced as a smooth temporal pattern.
P0114	STYLE=normal	Minimum temperature showed a more complex functional pattern characterized by a temporal reversal (Fig. 6D; Table 2). Higher minimum temperature was positively associated with DG early in the temporal domain, particularly from −70 to −51 days, whereas negative associations emerged later, from approximately −30 to −11 days. The overall functional association was retained across the main sensitivity analyses, although its strength varied when individual seasons were omitted (Figs. 7D and 8D). This pattern therefore appears more reproducible than the maximum-temperature or relative-humidity signals, but its changing direction across the temporal domain complicates biological interpretation.

P0123	STYLE=Heading 3	Cross-method concordance
P0124	STYLE=normal	Overall, log-transformed rainfall showed the strongest cross-method concordance, with a broad positive association centered approximately 60 to 40 days before evaluation. The functional model integrated contrast supported a broader positive region extending approximately to −31 days. The largest coefficients in both analyses were observed for log-transformed rainfall for the -50 to  -40 interval, +3.40 DG percentage points and the corresponding scalar-on-function integrated contrast +4.81 percentage points. Thus agreeing in direction and timing. Furthermore, the rainfall pattern was largely preserved after penalization. The REML-penalized functional sensitivity produced a similar process-level result, and despite including zero in the simultaneous confidence band (Fig. S6), integrated contrast preserved a similar window excluding zero (Fig. S7). 
P0125	STYLE=normal	Secondarily, minimum temperature also showed evidence of a temporally structured association, with a reversal from positive effects early to negative effects later in the domain, observed in both analyses. The broad-interval model supported a positive estimate during −70 to −61 days and a negative estimate during −20 to −11 days. The functional analysis identified positive integrated contrasts from −70 to −51 days and negative contrasts from −20 to −11 days, extended to -30 in the integrated contrast confidence interval. The penalized functional model retained positive integrated contrasts during −70 to −61 and −60 to −51 days, together with a negative contrast during −20 to −11 days (Fig. S7). However, penalization reduced the effective degrees of freedom, and the simultaneous ribbon included zero throughout the whole domain (Fig. S6).
P0127	STYLE=Heading 3	Residual diagnostics
P0128	STYLE=normal	Residual diagnostics showed similar patterns for the prespecified 10-day interval and scalar-on-function models (Figs. S2–S5). In the normal Q–Q plots, standardized residuals followed the reference line reasonably closely through the central portion of the distribution, with the clearest departures occurring in the upper tail (Figs. S3 and S5). A small number of large positive residuals indicated substantial underestimation of DG in several high-damage trials, suggesting that departures from normality were concentrated mainly among the most severe outcomes. Residual-versus-fitted plots showed most observations centered around zero, but suggested increasing dispersion with fitted DG in several models and some curvature in the smoothed residual trends (Figs. S2 and S4). These patterns are consistent with possible nonconstant variance and some limitation in the fitted mean structure, although behavior near the extremes was informed by relatively few observations. Overall, the diagnostics indicated that the models represented typical DG outcomes better than the most severe cases.

P0130	STYLE=Heading 2	Discussion
P0131	STYLE=normal	The main finding of this study was a broad rainfall-associated period during the reproductive phase, centered approximately 60 to 40 days before harvest. The consistency of this pattern across the prespecified interval and scalar-on-function analyses suggests that rainfall was associated with grain damage over an extended period rather than within a narrowly defined critical window. Minimum temperature showed a second, more complex temporal pattern, with a positive association earlier in the exposure domain and a negative association later in the season. In contrast, associations with relative humidity and maximum temperature were less consistently reproduced across analytical approaches and sensitivity analyses.
P0132	STYLE=normal	The rainfall signal is consistent with previous observations from tropical Brazil, where Lehner et al. (2025) reported approximately 1.7-fold greater cumulative precipitation during the reproductive stage in a late-planted soybean trial that also developed substantially more damaged grains. Because rainfall in that study was summarized across the entire reproductive period, however, the timing of the association could not be resolved. Our results refine that observation by identifying a more specific period, approximately 60 to 40 days before harvest, broadly corresponding to pod development and early seed filling, during which rainfall showed the strongest and most reproducible association with final grain damage. This timing is epidemiologically plausible because rainfall can promote splash dispersal of inoculum, prolong surface wetness, and favor infection and colonization. Experimental work with the Diaporthe complex showed that inoculum can be splash-dispersed from infected debris and establish latent infections in immature soybean tissues, including pods, before symptoms become apparent (Kmetz et al. 1979). Field studies have likewise associated rainfall and moisture during pod filling and seed maturation with subsequent Diaporthe seed infection and decay (Shortt et al. 1981; TeKrony et al. 1983), while more recent work indicates that warm, humid conditions and repeated precipitation during pod and seed development can favor latent infection and subsequent deterioration (Mengistu et al. 2010; Rupe et al. 2026). Thus, the broad rainfall-associated period identified here may represent conditions favorable for inoculum dispersal, infection, and establishment well before grain damage becomes evident at harvest. Because canopy wetness, pathogen abundance, and infection were not measured directly, however, the present analysis cannot distinguish among these processes, and the rainfall signal should be interpreted as evidence of a broader moisture-associated environment conducive to subsequent grain deterioration.
P0133	STYLE=normal	Minimum temperature showed a temporally structured but complex association with DG, with higher values associated with greater damage early in the exposure domain, immediately preceding and partly overlapping the main rainfall-associated period, and a negative association emerging later in the season. The early positive signal may be compatible with effects of relatively warm nighttime conditions on both host and pathogen processes, although daily minimum temperature should be regarded only as a proxy for nighttime thermal conditions. High night temperature during seed filling and maturation has been shown to reduce soybean seed germination and vigor independently of daytime temperature (Gibson and Mullen 1996), while high-temperature exposure can also increase seed discoloration and physical deterioration and alter seed metabolism, protein composition, and cellular storage structures (Keigley and Mullen 1986; Chebrolu et al. 2016; Krishnan et al. 2020). A pathogen-mediated mechanism is also plausible because infection by Diaporthe longicolla (formerly Phomopsis longicolla) is strongly temperature dependent, and favorable temperatures acting together with rainfall and prolonged surface wetness can promote infection (Rupe 1990; Rupe and Ferriss 1987). The partial temporal overlap between elevated minimum temperature and the rainfall-associated period therefore raises the possibility that thermal and moisture conditions act jointly or sequentially on infection, colonization, and subsequent seed deterioration. Combined high temperature and humidity during late seed development can also induce physiological and metabolic changes associated with pre-harvest seed deterioration (Wang et al. 2012). However, these mechanisms were not measured directly, and the later negative Tmin association indicates that temperature effects may differ across reproductive stages. The minimum-temperature pattern should therefore be viewed as a stage-dependent hypothesis that warrants independent evaluation.
P0134	STYLE=normal	A strength of this study was the use of a continuous damaged-grain response rather than a binary quality threshold. This preserved quantitative variation among trials and allowed weather associations to be expressed directly in DG percentage points. The temporal modeling framework also avoided relying solely on numerous ad hoc weather summaries. By combining seven prespecified 10-day intervals with a low-dimensional scalar-on-function model, we constrained the temporal complexity that could be inferred from a modest dataset while evaluating weather effects at two complementary temporal resolutions. The interval models provided readily interpretable period-specific associations, whereas scalar-on-function regression assessed whether broader smooth temporal patterns were supported without treating individual days as independent testing opportunities. 
P0135	STYLE=normal	Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. First, the approximately 72 field trials across four growing seasons provide a valuable multi-environment dataset but limited power to resolve subtle nonlinear responses, weather interactions, or cultivar-specific effects. The temporal patterns should therefore be regarded as candidate epidemiological signals rather than precisely defined biological windows. Second, weather conditions were obtained from ERA5 reanalysis. Although this ensured spatial and temporal consistency across locations, gridded data may not fully represent canopy-level conditions, particularly short-duration variation in temperature, humidity, rainfall exposure, and surface wetness. Field-level microclimate measurements would therefore be valuable for validating the atmospheric signatures identified here. A further limitation concerns etiology as pod and grain rot in Brazilian soybean fields is a syndromic outcome rather than a single pathogen-specific disease. Diaporthe ueckeri and D. longicolla can reproduce pod and grain rot symptoms experimentally (Nascimento et al. 2025), but surveys of symptomatic and asymptomatic tissues have recovered diverse fungal taxa, including Diaporthe, Fusarium, Colletotrichum, Cercospora, and Moniliophthora (Ramos et al. 2026). Detection or isolation alone does not establish that each taxon contributed to the observed damage. Consequently, DG may integrate pathogen infection, latent colonization, host physiological deterioration, and other forms of grain damage, and the weather associations identified here cannot be attributed to a single causal agent.
P0136	STYLE=normal	Overall, the most consistent temporal feature was a broad rainfall-associated period approximately 60 to 40 days before evaluation. Minimum temperature also showed a reproducible but more complex temporal structure, with a positive association earlier in the exposure domain, partly preceding and overlapping the rainfall-associated period, followed by a negative association later in the season. In contrast, maximum temperature and relative humidity were less consistently reproduced across analytical approaches. Taken together, these results support temporally structured environmental associations rather than a single sharply defined infection window or a demonstrated causal mechanism. Rainfall during the early-to-middle retrospective domain emerges as the strongest candidate for prospective validation, while the stage-dependent minimum-temperature pattern represents a second hypothesis of particular interest. The temporal signatures identified here provide a biologically grounded framework for targeted inoculation experiments, canopy-microclimate studies, and validation in larger independent datasets.
