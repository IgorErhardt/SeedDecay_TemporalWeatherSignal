P012	[Heading 2]	Major issues
P013	[Heading 3]	1. Trials are not independent
P014	[normal]	Figure 1 shows 22 trials in one ERA5 cell and 14 in another, yet the duplicate rule merged only 2 trials (72 → 70). The "cluster" bootstrap is therefore effectively a trial bootstrap, and the interval models use naive t-based CIs. Trials sharing a cell and season share weather, inoculum and management, so the effective n is well below 72.
P015	[normal]	What to do: cluster at cell × season, use the same clustered inference for both methods, and report the number of clusters.

# functional model bootstrap: resample clusters within season
boot_ids <- function(d) {
  d |> distinct(season, clust) |>
    group_by(season) |> slice_sample(prop = 1, replace = TRUE) |>
    mutate(rep = row_number()) |> ungroup() |>
    left_join(d, by = c("season", "clust"), relationship = "many-to-many")
}

P018	[Heading 3]	2. The harvest anchor is confounded with sowing date and phenology
P019	[normal]	Season is a fixed effect, so the signal is within-season, and much of that contrast comes from planting-date trials. Sowing date shifts weather, phenology and cycle length together. With 93–137 days from sowing to harvest, "−50 days" is a different stage in different trials. Harvest date is also partly endogenous, because rain delays harvest and delayed harvest raises DG. That may explain why −10 to −1 shows nothing.

P022	[Heading 3]	3. The functional shapes come largely from the basis
P023	[normal]	A cubic B-spline with 4 df over the whole domain has no interior knot when an intercept column is included, so β(t) is a global cubic polynomial. That basis can only make one hump (rainfall) or one S-shape (Tmin). Under REML penalization the Tmin reversal collapses to a nearly straight line with the band covering zero everywhere (Fig. S6D). It should not be called "reproducible".
P024	[normal]	The cross-method agreement criterion is also asymmetric. The 4-df curve cannot represent the RH and Tmax alternations, so its failure to reproduce them is not evidence against them. Conversely, eight correlated adjacent intervals entered together are the classic setup for seesawing signs. The RH and Tmax alternations are probably collinearity artifacts, and the paper should argue that directly.

P029	[Heading 3]	5. Incremental R² is in-sample and optimistic
P030	[normal]	Under the null, adding p predictors with n = 72 and 4 season terms gains about p / (n − 4) in R². That is about 0.12 for the 8-interval models (observed 0.25–0.27) and 0.06 for the functional models (observed about 0.15). Tmax SoF (0.057) is at the null expectation. Report adjusted R², a permutation null, and leave-one-season-out prediction.

P039	[Heading 3]	Response model
P040	[normal]	DG is a bounded, right-skewed percentage, and the residual plots show variance growing with the fitted value. Gaussian identity plus a log1p sensitivity is acceptable. A log-link model with robust SEs is more natural and gives multiplicative effects that are easier to discuss. Sample mass ranged from 25 to 125 g, so precision varies too.
